WHAASCO
West Hartford African American Social & Cultural Organization
Who We Are
We, the members of the West Hartford African American Social and Cultural Organization (WHAASCO), are a non-profit organization established to support, encourage, and enhance the social, cultural, and educational well-being of African American families and the broader community.
Our Mission
Our mission is to promote unity, cultural awareness, and community involvement by providing programs and activities that strengthen families, support children, and foster pride in African American heritage and history.
Purpose
The purpose of WHAASCO is to:
Provide social, cultural, and educational opportunities for African American families
Promote positive self-image, self-awareness, and cultural pride
Encourage parental involvement in education and community life
Support youth development through enrichment activities
Serve as a resource and advocate for families within the community
Programs and Activities
WHAASCO offers a variety of programs designed to support families and children, including but not limited to:
Educational enrichment activities
Cultural and heritage-based events
Family-oriented social gatherings
Community outreach and engagement
Youth-focused programs that promote academic success and personal growth
Community Focus
Our organization is committed to building strong relationships within the West Hartford community and surrounding areas. We work collaboratively with families, educators, and community partners to create an inclusive environment that values diversity, respect, and mutual support.
Vision
We envision a thriving community where African American families are empowered, children are supported in reaching their full potential, and cultural heritage is celebrated and preserved.

Support Our Mission
Your support helps WHAASCO strengthen families, empower youth, and celebrate African American culture. By giving your time, resources, or financial support, you help us provide educational enrichment, cultural programming, and community connections that make a lasting impact.

Formation of West Hartford African American Social & Cultural Organization
In 1979 a small group of African American residents organized a protest to bring attention to a racist organization’s rally at a local retail center in West Hartford. Also, parents of African American children in town were expressing concerns over common experiences their children were experiencing in school, particularly in the areas of teacher attitude, school receptivity and parent involvement.
To share their experiences and to discuss the stresses of living in a predominately white upper middle class suburb, African American residents felt the need to meet other African American residents on a continuing basis and to become acquainted with each other. Strategies were developed to cope with those stresses as well as friendships. Mrs. Ida McKinney was the first WHAASCO president.
History of Community Advocacy
·         Assumed a significant role in advocating for hiring qualified African American personnel for town employment including school teachers, fire fighters, police officers, etc.
·         Invited and interacted with town officials where members’ concerns relative to issues of African Americans were discussed.
·         Discussion of school curricula in regards to racial & cultural sensitivity and awareness.
Example of one solo activity of WHAASCO
    In 2003-20004 WHAASCO members raised money to replace the significantly eroded headstone of Bristow, the only known African American to have a headstone in the oldest cemetery in West Hartford, CT. Bristow was an enslaved person who purchased his own freedom from Thomas Hart Hooker for 60 pounds in 1776. Bristow was 44 years old.
 	The high level of publicity and discussion in town around the headstone replacement efforts made Bristow’s name a household word which laid the foundation for the naming of the newly constructed middle school, Bristow Middle School.
Some of the numerous program topics presented over the years
·         “Public Education: Quality & Diversity- What does it mean for African American children in West Hartford & CT?”
·         “Forgotten Souls: African Americans in Hartford’s Ancient Burying Ground”
·         “The significance of the Amistad Incident to CT & U.S. History”
·         “Southern Migration & the Transformation of Black Hartford, 1915-1949”
·         “An Afternoon with a Tuskegee Airman”, interview with Mr. Connie Nappier, Jr., a Tuskegee Airman & 2007 recipient of the Congressional Gold Medal
·         Video on the life of Frederick Douglass followed by a discussion
·         Co-sponsored Candidates Night at West Hartford Town Hall Auditorium
Social & Cultural Activities
·         Cookouts, holiday parties, restaurant trips, acknowledgements of significant life events
Food & Gift Donations
·         The West Hartford Food Pantry, Hillcrest Area Neighborhood Outreach Center (West Hartford)
·         The Village for Families & Children, Inc., (Hartford), St. Agnes Home, Inc., (West Hartford)

A Tribute to Earl Exum
WHAASCO’s past president who served until his passing in October 2023. 
 
Igbo African Proverb that embodied Earl:
“If a man cooks for his community, his community will finish the food, but if a community cooks for a man, he can never finish the food”. 
In other words, the greater good of the community is always above that of one man. 
 
Below are just a few examples of the community reach of just one man as WHAASCO’s commanding leader and beyond.
 
Connecting Communities
·        As Whaasco’s past President, he grew our membership and broadened our community engagement.
·        He was instrumental in bringing to fruition United with Jazz through engagement with the West Hartford Police department, The Town of West Hartford and Bridge. He wanted to bridge the gap between the community and the police in a social setting rather than a crisis.
·        He understood the importance of installing a memorial in honor of Lemual Haynes on the grounds of 1st Church on our town green.  There’s no better way to foster inclusive community engagement than to educate the residents of WeHa on the accomplishments of an African American Man, who was born here in our town in 1755.  In doing so he established a community legacy
·        If you visit the New England Air Museum to view the Tuskegee Airman Exhibit, you will understand Earls passion to establish these men in New England’s history of aviation.  He understood connecting aviation enthusiast, with the historic contribution of black airman, along WITH an aviation company for which he was an executive in order to bring the project to fruition. 
·        He also understood the breeding ground of future black leaders at Howard University.  He connected with the university community in DC and joined the board of advisors for the Howard University Business School.  He left a legacy of mentoring future leaders as a pipeline to continue his DE&I initiatives to include people of color, women, and people of diverse backgrounds in positions of leadership.
·        We can only imagine Earls’ civic reach if he had the opportunity to complete his work on the Board of the Hartford Foundation for Public Giving for which he was to be installed later in 2023.
Closing African proverb - “Let us be the people of tomorrow”. 
It’s a proverb of hope.  Yesterday was yesterday, today is today, but tomorrow is inexhaustible.  We all know that Earl had endless hope for better communities.
